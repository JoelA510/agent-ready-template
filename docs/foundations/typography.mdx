# Typography

Type does more work than any other design token in this system. A fresh template starts with
almost no established brand — no library of marketing copy or UI to inherit a voice from —
which means the typeface choices are, for now, most of what "the brand" visibly is. That's
exactly why this page treats the display/heading typeface as an open decision rather than a
settled one: get the type wrong and there's no other visual signal to correct the impression.

Everything below the options section — the scale, the line-height and letter-spacing
tokens, the sans-vs-serif usage rule — is settled and safe to build against today. The
open question is narrower and specific: **which typeface pairs with Inter for display and
heading type.**

## The one thing that isn't in question: Inter for body text

Inter is the body/UI font in all three options below and isn't being reconsidered here. It's
a safe, neutral default for a template — one of the most extensively hinted, screen-tested
typefaces available for interface work — and self-hosting it via `@fontsource-variable/inter`
means no third-party font CDN request on every page load. Every option in this document is really a question about one thing:
what pairs with Inter for display and heading type.

## The options for display/heading type

This system self-hosts four fonts specifically so this comparison can be made with real,
rendered type rather than descriptions of it: Inter (settled, above), and three
candidates for the display/heading role — Fraunces, Almarai, and Source Serif 4. Each is
shown below at the same size and weight so the differences are visible, not just asserted.

### Option 1 — Fraunces + Inter (current working default)

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-lg border-2 border-brand-700 p-6 dark:border-brand-500">
  <p className="font-sans text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wide text-brand-700 dark:text-brand-500 mb-3">Implemented now in src/styles/globals.css</p>
  <p className="font-serif text-4xl font-semibold mb-1">Acme Inc.</p>
  <p className="font-serif text-xl italic text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-400">We help good people build good organizations.</p>
</div>

Fraunces is a warm, soft-contrast serif with old-style, slightly quirky detailing —
noticeably distinct from a workhorse text serif. It's become a common choice in recent
professional-services and hospitality rebrands precisely because it reads as considered
and editorial without tipping into stuffiness.

**Why this is the recommendation:** of the three options, Fraunces creates the strongest
visual separation from the Inter body text — it doesn't just look like "Inter's serif
cousin," it looks like a deliberate typographic decision. That counts for a lot if your
organization's logo wordmark is a distinctive classical serif rather than a generic one:
display type that's merely "a bigger, serif-shaped version of the body font" would
undersell that mark, and Fraunces is the option most likely to feel like it belongs next to
it. (Replace this reasoning with your own wordmark's character when you make the call.)

**The honest tradeoff:** Fraunces' warmth and slightly quirky old-style details are a
stronger personality than a text serif like Source Serif 4. That's the point, but it also
means it's the option furthest from "safe" — it will read as a specific choice to anyone
who notices type at all, not a neutral one.

### Option 2 — Almarai + Inter

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-lg border border-neutral-200 p-6 dark:border-neutral-700">
  <p className="font-sans text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wide text-neutral-500 dark:text-neutral-400 mb-3">A sans that matches an existing sans-first brand</p>
  <p className="font-almarai text-4xl font-bold mb-1">Acme Inc.</p>
  <p className="font-almarai text-xl text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-400">We help good people build good organizations.</p>
</div>

Almarai is a geometric sans — rounded, friendly, low-contrast strokes. It's the option to
reach for if your organization already ships a sans-first brand: choosing it would mean the
marketing site and every product surface share one heading typeface with zero translation
between them.

**Why it's worth serious consideration:** full visual consistency with an existing sans-first
brand, today, with no rebrand risk. If the top priority is "match what already ships" rather
than "make the best independent typographic decision," this is the answer.

**The honest tradeoff:** Almarai is a sans, so it doesn't echo a serif logo wordmark's
character at all — the wordmark and the heading type would be making two different
typographic arguments. It also tends to read a little more casual and startup-adjacent than
the gravitas many B2B brands reach for in large display type. That may be exactly right for a
younger or more playful brand, but it's worth naming plainly rather than glossing over.

### Option 3 — Source Serif 4 + Inter

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-lg border border-neutral-200 p-6 dark:border-neutral-700">
  <p className="font-sans text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wide text-neutral-500 dark:text-neutral-400 mb-3">The safe middle-ground pick</p>
  <p className="font-source-serif text-4xl font-semibold mb-1">Acme Inc.</p>
  <p className="font-source-serif text-xl italic text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-400">We help good people build good organizations.</p>
</div>

Source Serif 4 is a conservative, classic transitional serif — closer in spirit to the
refined, classical character of a traditional serif wordmark than Fraunces' warmer, quirkier
personality, and a genuine serif (unlike Almarai).

**Why it's the middle ground:** it echoes a serif wordmark without asking anyone to
sign off on a distinctive, opinionated typeface. It's also the most generic of the three —
less distinctive than Fraunces, more traditionally "serif" and less brand-consistent than
matching an existing sans. This is the pick if the room can't agree and wants the option
least likely to be wrong, rather than the option most likely to be memorable.

### Comparison at a glance

| | Option 1: Fraunces + Inter | Option 2: Almarai + Inter | Option 3: Source Serif 4 + Inter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Status | **Current working default** — implemented | Matches an existing sans-first brand | Not implemented; available for comparison |
| Character | Warm soft-serif, old-style, editorial | Geometric sans, rounded, friendly | Classic transitional serif, conservative |
| Echoes a serif wordmark? | Yes — distinct serif personality | No — it's a sans | Yes — closer to classical/refined |
| Distinctiveness vs. Inter body | Highest of the three | Lowest (same sans family logic as body) | Moderate |
| Best argument for it | Strongest "this is special" signal for display type | Zero-friction consistency with an existing sans brand | Safe, echoes a serif wordmark, low risk |
| Best argument against it | Most opinionated; needs real buy-in | Doesn't reflect a serif wordmark's character; can read casual | Most generic; least memorable of the three |

**How to switch:** the working default is a two-line change, not a structural one. It
lives in `tokens/core/typography.json`'s `font.family.serif` value (feeding
`--font-family-serif`), which `src/styles/globals.css`'s `@theme inline` block exposes as
`--font-serif` — swap the font stack there, run `npm run tokens:build`, and every
`font-serif` usage across the system updates. Nothing about component code, the type
scale, or any other token changes.

This is exactly the kind of call this system is set up to leave open rather than
force: it's a brand decision as much as a technical one, and it should be made by whoever
owns Acme's brand, with the three real, rendered options in front of them — not
defaulted silently because Fraunces happened to be implemented first.

## Type scale

The scale is a fixed set of nine sizes. It isn't part of the options discussion above —
this is the same numeric scale regardless of which display typeface wins.

| Token | rem | px | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `text-xs` | 0.75rem | 12px | Fine print, timestamps, disclaimers |
| `text-sm` | 0.875rem | 14px | Secondary UI text, labels, table cells, captions |
| `text-md` (`text-base`) | 1rem | 16px | Body copy default — the size most text on a page should be |
| `text-lg` | 1.125rem | 18px | Emphasized body copy, lead paragraphs |
| `text-xl` | 1.25rem | 20px | Small headings, card titles |
| `text-2xl` | 1.5rem | 24px | Section headings |
| `text-3xl` | 1.875rem | 30px | Page headings |
| `text-4xl` | 2.25rem | 36px | Prominent page headings, feature callouts |
| `text-5xl` | 3rem | 48px | Hero and marketing display type |

Two sizes are deliberately absent between `4xl` and `5xl` and above `5xl`: this scale is
built for product UI, where anything larger than a hero headline belongs to a marketing
page's own layout, not a shared component token.

## Line height

Line height is a separate token from size — pick it based on how the text is being read,
not how big it is.

| Token | Value | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `leading-tight` | 1.1 | Large display headings (`3xl` and up) — at that size, generous line spacing looks disconnected rather than readable |
| `leading-snug` | 1.3 | Smaller headings and card titles — enough air to separate wrapped lines without floating apart |
| `leading-normal` | 1.5 | Body copy default — the standard for comfortable multi-line reading |
| `leading-relaxed` | 1.65 | Longer-form reading passages (long-form documentation, help content) where extra spacing measurably reduces reading fatigue |

## Letter spacing

| Token | Value | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `tracking-tight` | -0.02em | Large display headings, where default spacing can look slightly loose at big sizes |
| `tracking-normal` | 0em | Everything else — body copy, UI labels, most headings |
| `tracking-wide` | 0.02em | All-caps labels and eyebrows (like the "Implemented now" labels above) — capital letters need a touch more room to avoid feeling cramped |

## When to use serif vs. sans

One rule, applied consistently regardless of which display option above is chosen:

- **`font-sans` (Inter) is the default for everything functional.** Body copy, buttons,
  form labels, table data, navigation, error messages, empty states — anything the user
  reads to *do* something, not to be persuaded by. If you're not sure which font a piece
  of UI text should use, it's `font-sans`.
- **`font-serif` (or whichever display option is chosen) is reserved for genuine display
  and editorial headings** — page titles, section headings on marketing and about pages,
  pull quotes. It should never appear in body copy, form fields, table cells, or any
  other UI chrome. The moment a serif shows up somewhere transactional, it stops reading
  as "considered" and starts reading as "inconsistent."

This mirrors GOV.UK's typography guidance: use one typeface for reading and interaction,
and reserve any second typeface for the small number of places where a shift in register
is doing real communicative work — not as decoration sprinkled through the interface.

## Reference systems this leans on

- **GOV.UK Design System** — the rationale-first structure of this page (state the
  reasoning, not just the rule) follows GOV.UK's convention of never asserting "do this"
  without "because...".
- **IBM Carbon** — Carbon's pattern of documenting several supported options for a single
  decision, rather than one mandate, is the model for presenting three real typeface
  options here instead of picking silently and moving on.
- **Shopify Polaris** — pairing concrete before/after examples (the rendered swatches
  above) with the written guidance, rather than describing type choices in the abstract.
