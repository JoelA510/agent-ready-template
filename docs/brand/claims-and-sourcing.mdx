# Claims & Sourcing

A **claim** is anything in the product or its marketing that a reader could hold you to:

- a number ("cuts review time by 40%", "99.9% uptime");
- a comparison or superlative ("faster than spreadsheets", "the easiest", "#1");
- a guarantee or absolute ("never loses your work", "unlimited", "always on");
- a security, privacy, or compliance status ("secure", "encrypted", "HIPAA compliant",
  "SOC 2");
- a testimonial, a quote, or a customer's logo;
- what an automated or AI feature can do ("accurate", "understands your documents").

Voice and tone decide how a sentence sounds. This page decides whether it's allowed to exist. A
claim nobody can back is a bug: in the product it misleads the person relying on it, and in
marketing it's a legal exposure. The rules below are the same standard the engineering side
holds itself to (no success reported for work that didn't happen), applied to copy.

## The rules

1. **Every claim has a source you could show a skeptic.** That means a measurement with its
   method and date, a signed certification or audit report, a contract term, or a customer's
   written permission. If there's no source, rewrite the sentence as something you can back, or
   cut it.
2. **Every claim has an owner and a review date**, in the claims register below. Claims decay:
   a benchmark from two releases ago isn't evidence now, and a certification lapses.
3. **Security and compliance words are legal statements.** "Compliant", "certified",
   "secure", "private", and the names of standards appear only when the control or
   certification exists and its owner (legal, security) has signed off. Prefer saying what you
   do ("files are encrypted at rest and in transit") over a label ("bank-grade security").
4. **Numbers say what was measured, on what, and when.** Name the baseline ("2x faster than
   exporting to CSV", not "2x faster"), round honestly ("about 40%"), and don't average away
   the cases where it's worse.
5. **Superlatives and absolutes need proof or go.** "Best", "fastest", "always", "never",
   "guaranteed", and "unlimited" are claims; most can be replaced by the specific thing that's
   true.
6. **Describe what automated and AI features do, not how smart they are.** Say where they can
   be wrong, and what the person should check ("Summaries can miss details. Check the source
   before you send.").
7. **Testimonials and logos need written permission**, a real quote, and attribution the
   customer approved.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Invoices sync every 15 minutes. | Real-time sync. *(it isn't)* |
| Files are encrypted at rest and in transit. | Bank-grade security. *(no source, and it means nothing)* |
| Teams in our 2026 pilot closed projects 30% faster (12 teams, measured over 8 weeks). | Close projects 30% faster. *(faster than what, for whom, measured how?)* |
| Summaries are generated automatically. Check the source before you rely on one. | Our AI understands your documents. |
| Trusted by 200 firms. *(with the count's source and date in the register)* | Trusted by thousands. |

## The claims register

Keep one table, owned by whoever owns the product copy, and add a row in the same change that
adds a claim. The placeholder brand ships none: a real product starts with the claims on its
first landing page and in its first onboarding screen.

| Claim | Where it appears | Source | Owner | Verified | Review by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Invoices sync every 15 minutes.* | *Settings → Integrations, pricing page* | *Sync scheduler config, `jobs/sync.ts`* | *Integrations lead* | *2026-09-01* | *2027-03-01* |

A target project that isn't a fork of this repo gets the same register in the content-guide
skeleton (`templates/project/docs/content-guide.md`).
